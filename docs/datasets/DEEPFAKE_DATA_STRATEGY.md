## 1. Architectural Strategy: Two-Branch Synthetic Media System

Synthetic media encountered during crises arrives in multiple distinct modalities, each requiring domain-appropriate forensic processing:
1. **Manipulated Video / Deepfakes:** Facial replacements, reenactments, and temporal video splices.
2. **AI-Generated Synthetic Images:** Photorealistic synthetic imagery generated by latent diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Midjourney) or GANs.

To address these modalities with scientific fidelity, CrisisGuard adopts a **Two-Branch Synthetic-Media Architecture**:

```
                    SYNTHETIC MEDIA
                           |
              +------------+------------+
              |                         |
              v                         v
        VIDEO FORENSICS            IMAGE FORENSICS
              |                         |
              v                         v
   DFD-derived sample (Video)        CIFAKE (Image)
              |                         |
              v                         v
       Video/frame model          Image model
              |                         |
              +------------+------------+
                           |
                           v
                  Unified Media Risk
                           |
                           v
                 synthetic_media_risk
```

## 2. Branch Roles & Dataset Provenance

### 2.1 Video Forensics Branch: Google DFD-Derived Controlled Development Sample
* **Data Source:** **Google DFD-derived controlled development sample** (sourced from Google & Jigsaw / TUM FaceForensics++).
* **Local Scope:** 9 curated files totaling 12.39 MB (canonical animated sequences `deepfakedetection.gif` and `DDD_samples.gif`, actor frame pairs `ex_original_actors.png` and `ex_deepfakedetection.png`, binary mask, and official split indices `train.json`, `val.json`, `test.json`).
* **Governance Note:** These 9 local files constitute a **controlled development sample** used for pipeline validation and feature extraction development. They do **not** represent the complete multi-hundred-gigabyte Google DFD dataset.
* **Role:** Video/deepfake detection development, frame sampling, facial bounding box alignment, and facial manipulation artifact extraction.

### 2.2 Image Forensics Branch: CIFAKE AI-Generated Image Benchmark
* **Data Source:** **CIFAKE: Real and AI-Generated Synthetic Images** (Bird & Lotfi, IEEE Access 2024).
* **Local Scope:** 500 authentic benchmark images (250 photographic CIFAR-10 real images and 250 Stable Diffusion v1.4 synthetic images, deterministically sampled with `seed=42`).
* **Role:** Dedicated **AI-generated image detection development, image forensic feature extraction, image-model evaluation, and synthetic-image risk scoring**.
* **Governance Note:** CIFAKE is an **image forensics benchmark**, **NOT** a video or deepfake benchmark. It is evaluated independently on image synthesis detection.

## 3. Experimental Claims & Evaluation Protocol

1. **Independent Branch Evaluation:**
   * DFD-derived video branch and CIFAKE image branch are evaluated independently. Their outputs are normalized into a common synthetic-media risk representation.
   * Model outputs are calibrated into a bounded synthetic-media risk score in $[0.0, 1.0]$.
2. **No False Cross-Dataset Generalization Claims:**
   * CIFAKE is not a direct cross-dataset generalization test of a DFD-trained video detector, as their modalities (video face-swap vs. 2D diffusion generation) and generation mechanisms differ fundamentally.
   * Direct cross-dataset generalization between video face-swaps and diffusion images is not claimed.
3. **Optional Exploratory Transfer:**
   * A cross-modal/domain-transfer experiment may be explored as a future research question, but it must NOT be claimed as ordinary generalization.

## 4. Canonical Unified Output Schema

Both media branches produce identical canonical structured records, enabling consistent downstream Big Data ingestion across Kafka, Spark Structured Streaming, MLlib, and Hive:

```json
{
  "content_id": "cifake_img_00250_synthetic",
  "media_type": "image/jpeg",
  "synthetic_probability": 0.942,
  "synthetic_risk": 0.885,
  "model_version": "resnet50_image_forensics_v1.0",
  "prediction_timestamp": "2026-09-27T00:30:00Z"
}
```

### Schema Field Definitions:
* `content_id` *(string)*: Unique identifier for the media asset.
* `media_type` *(string)*: MIME type (e.g., `video/mp4`, `image/jpeg`, `image/png`).
* `synthetic_probability` *(float, [0.0, 1.0])*: Raw classifier confidence of synthetic generation or manipulation.
* `synthetic_risk` *(float, [0.0, 1.0])*: Context-calibrated risk weighting reflecting potential weaponization impact.
* `model_version` *(string)*: Model architecture and checkpoint identifier.
* `prediction_timestamp` *(string)*: ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of inference.

## 5. CrisisGuard Downstream Pipeline Integration

### Synthetic Media Risk as a Predictive Feature
In CrisisGuard, synthetic media outputs enter the event processing stream as **features**:
* `synthetic_media_probability`
* `synthetic_media_risk`
* `media_type`

**Critical Operational Boundary:**
* Synthetic media risk **does not directly determine whether an emergency crisis report is true or false**. A real disaster event (e.g., severe flooding) may co-occur with recycled or synthetic imagery, or genuine eyewitness reports may circulate alongside synthetic media.
* The final **Emergency Dispatch Priority Index (EDPI)** computed in Spark MLlib combines multiple orthogonal dimensions:
  1. **Crisis Severity:** Physical damage classifications from CrisisMMD.
  2. **Urgency:** Life-safety urgency extracted from HumAID humanitarian text.
  3. **Propagation Characteristics:** Burst velocity and windowed event count from Spark Streaming.
  4. **Graph Influence:** PageRank amplifier scores and coordination bot clusters from GraphX.
  5. **Synthetic-Media Risk:** Forensic risk score from the two-branch media pipeline.
  6. **Road Accessibility:** Shortest path network reachability to depots computed via GraphX on OpenStreetMap.
  7. **Resource Proximity:** Physical distance to nearest relief stations and emergency responders.
